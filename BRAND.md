**Category:** The deployment layer for AI in the economy

## The conjecture

Feld is drafted from a public post by Aaron Levie (@levie). The post’s argument, which the site treats as a founding text and credits by name, is this:

Enterprises are about to need a deployment layer, not another demonstration. The work of changing a real workflow is heavier than a keynote admits. The applied layer of AI will look like software and like agents, and it will also look like new services firms.

That work has a shape. Legacy systems have to be moved into reach. Data has to be organized and access has to be redesigned. Software a company already runs has to be connected to agents in ways the original contract did not draw. Workflows have to be reengineered so an agent has a job. Human-in-the-loop has to be decided per process, not as a slogan. Evals have to be generated from the real work and then kept. The whole install has to be updated when new models and new capabilities arrive. The list is probably longer than that.

This is a different engagement from software implementation. A known category of software was installed, and then the vendor stepped back and the customer ran it. An agent engagement delivers work augmentation inside a process. The firm is deploying work output, with all the complexity that implies: a different implementation, and a different way of staying.

The result is room for new firms, and for old ones that can change. Approaches will differ by industry, by the size of the company, and by the particular problem inside the building. Some traditional systems integrators will modernize. Some will not. New entrants will be founded while the window is open. It is a good time to be a forward-deployed engineer, or a firm made of them.

## Position

Feld installs agents into work a company already does, and remains on the install as the models change.

The firm sells neither a model nor a seat. It sells a placed change in a process: a system the agent can reach, access an auditor can read, a binding to software the company already paid for, a redrawn workflow, a named human at the point of release, evals with an owner and a decay date, and a return visit when the model weather shifts.

## Who it is for

Operators who have already seen the demonstration. Chief information officers, chief operating officers, chiefs of AI, plant and claims and intake leaders, and the rare integrator who intends to adapt. The site refuses the browser who wants a chatbot on the homepage and a price in the corner.

## How the offer is cut

Levie’s cut is the site map.

- **By the problem inside the company.** Eight labors: Passage, Access, Bindings, Redraw, The Loop, Living evals, Weather, and Unlisted.
- **By industry.** The floor, not the sector slogan: banking, insurance, health-system operations, plants, energy, freight, retail operations, civic institutions, firms of record.
- **By size.** The global enterprise, the upper mid-market, the holding company.

Around those, the site shows the instruments (shadow week, the handback, attestation), the commercial shape, the people, and four composite field records.

## The eight labors

| Labor | Plain name | What “done” means |
|---|---|---|
| Passage | Legacy, lifted into reach | An agent can read and, where allowed, write the system of record without a scrape and a prayer |
| Access | Data, described and permissioned | Someone can answer who may read, draft, release, and train |
| Bindings | Existing software, joined | The system of record, the system people actually use, and the unofficial system (the inbox, the workbook) each have a deliberate connection |
| Redraw | The workflow, redesigned | The agent has a job, a queue, and a visible way to be wrong |
| The Loop | Human-in-the-loop, per process | Release, review, exception, escalation, and night each have a named person |
| Living evals | Evals from the real work | A set of cases, an owner, a decay date, a threshold that pages a human |
| Weather | The install, continued | A log of model changes, what was retested, and what was refused |
| Unlisted | The longer list | Incentives, names, freezes, regulators, the person who knows the exception |

## Commercial shape

Priced as an install plus a year of weather, not as seats. A single process in one country is a different shape from a doctrine across a group of companies. Figures, when they appear, are structures, not a rate card. The pricing page is a letter.

## Facts the copy may rely on

- No headquarters. Pods sit in the customer’s building for the cartography and the shadow week, and return for weather.
- A pod is small: one forward-deployed lead, one cartographer or eval keeper, one industry principal on a lighter cadence, security reviewing the binding before any write-access.
- The firm is model-neutral. It will recommend a model for a task and refuse one that retains what it must not, or one that the evals will not carry.
- “Done” is a living eval and a named keeper inside the customer, not a go-live.
- The firm will decline work. The refusal page is part of the offer. See the journal essay and the begin form’s question: “What must never be automated?”

## Name and mark

Feld is the field in forward-deployed. Say it as one syllable, like “felt” with a d.

The mark is the word FELD in the display face, with a surveyor’s tick inside the counter of the D: a short right angle, 12 degrees of overshoot, in patina by day and phosphor by night. No symbol-only logo below 24px. No icon of a brain, a node graph, a sparkle, or a robot.
