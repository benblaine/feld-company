### The letter

From Adaeze Okonkwo, managing principal.

You are used to buying software by the seat, the environment, or the year of access. Those are coherent prices for a tool the company is enabled by. They are a strange price for work output in a process. A seat does not describe a batch window, a keeper, or a refusal.

We price two things.

The install, which is the map through the first narrow release or the honest decision to wait. Cartography alone, if that is where we should stop, is priced as its own piece so that stopping is not a commercial failure.

The year of weather, which is the cadence, the log, the return when the model changes, and the call you are entitled to make. It is not a bank of vague advisory hours. If a year passes with no material change, you still paid for the watching, and the log will show the watching happened.

The shape changes with the cut you already saw on the site. A single process in one country, in a company whose CIO also resets access, is a different engagement from a doctrine across a group, proved in three countries. We will say which shape we think you are in, before we say a figure. We will not publish the figure here, because a figure without the process is how both sides waste a quarter.

What will not appear in the figure: a charge per draft, which teaches the wrong haste; a charge per model token passed through, which teaches us to be verbose; a discount for letting us train on your files. Training is a permission, asked for case by case. It is not a pricing lever.

If a firm quotes you a seat for this work, ask them who the keeper is.

Adaeze
