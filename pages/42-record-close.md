**Dek:** A mid-size finance team. The close runs ten working days. Day four is when the reconciliations either become a list or become a weekend. We were hired into day four, not into "finance transformation."

### The break list

The agent reads the two sources the controller already trusts, and drafts the list of breaks: what differs, by how much in their own units, and which side moved since yesterday. It does not propose a journal entry. The accountant releases the list. The controller remains the person who decides an entry. That refusal — no proposed entry — was the condition of the engagement. It made the install smaller and the trust possible.

### The unofficial workbook

There was one. Of course there was one. It held the three accounts the system describes badly. Access named it, gave it an owner, and bound it read-only with an expiry on the far side of the audit. The agent may use it and must cite it, so a break that comes from the workbook looks different from a break that comes from the ledger. Citation is the whole honesty of the draft.

### The weekend

In the composite, the weekend still happened once, in the first close, because we entered on day four of a close already in motion. The second close, the list was in the controller's hands at noon on day four. We do not write a number on "hours returned." The controller's sentence was enough for them: "I could see the breaks without hunting." We quote a composite sentence, and we do not dress it as a testimonial with a logo.

### Weather during a freeze

A model change landed inside the close. The log says Held. The previous model stayed through the sign-off. Weather does not get to be interesting during a close. The doctrine and the energy page agree, for different storms.

### Close

The next record is an outage desk. The sentence matters there for a different reader.
