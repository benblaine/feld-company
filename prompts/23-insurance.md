## Copy

**Browser title:** Insurance — Feld Company

**Eyebrow:** Industries · Insurance

**Headline:** The note still leaves under their name.

**Dek:** A claims floor is a place where fluency is cheap and a wrong coverage is expensive. We install the draft of the reserve note, the refusal to draft, and the desk where unsure files wait for a person.

### The note

The agent reads the file, the photos' metadata, and the prior ruling. It prepares the note in the house voice. It lists what it does not know. It does not reopen a coverage that was ruled out. The handler edits or accepts, and releases. The attestation keeps the draft, the edit, and the name.

This is clock B on the enablement page, and it is the composite record later in the quire. We repeat it because repetition is how a firm shows it means one thing.

### The first notice

At first notice of loss, the temptation is to be helpful and wide. We would rather be narrow. Capture what the binding can store cleanly, ask for the photograph once, and do not promise a settlement in a sentence the eval has not earned. Speed at the door is how a firm buys a year of bad files.

### Fairness, operationally

Similar files should receive similar drafts. The eval's ugly cases exist to watch for drift between, for example, the carefully documented claimant and the claimant whose photos arrived late. If those diverge, the queue pauses. Fairness here is not a campaign. It is a diff.

### Close

If your floor still pastes notes into a pane nobody owns, you are on clock A. We know the way across.
