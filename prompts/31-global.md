## Copy

**Browser title:** Global enterprise — Feld Company

**Meta description:** How an install works in a company of tens of thousands: one process, three countries, a doctrine that can travel without pretending the floors are the same.

**Eyebrow:** Scale · The global enterprise

**Headline:** One process. Three countries. Then a doctrine.

**Dek:** A company of fifty thousand people does not adopt an agent. A process inside it does. The art is choosing the process that can travel, and refusing the fantasy that a template is a rollout.

### The first country

We pick the floor where the release decision is already named and the system of record is almost in reach. Not the country whose managing director is most enthusiastic. Enthusiasm is not a control. The first country is where the eval can be fed real files within the first month.

### The second country

The doctrine meets a different legal entity, a different freeze calendar, and a different unofficial workbook. Cartography is repeated, shorter than the first time and more skeptical. Anything we hoped was universal and is not gets written into the doctrine as a local page, not smoothed away.

### The third country

By now we know whether we have a doctrine or a single success story. The third country is the test. If it requires the original pod in full, we do not have a doctrine. We stop and say so. Stopping is cheaper than a global program of exceptions.

### What does not scale, on purpose

The keeper is local. The release name is local. The language of the applicant or the customer is local. The weather log is central. Confusing these is how a global program becomes ungoverned in nine languages.

### Close

Bring the process, not the transformation office. If the process cannot be pointed at on a map of the company, it is not ready for three countries. It is barely ready for one.
