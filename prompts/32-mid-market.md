## Copy

**Browser title:** Upper mid-market — Feld Company

**Meta description:** Installs for a company of hundreds, where the CIO is also the person resetting access, and the keeper we leave behind has to be someone they can actually hire.

**Eyebrow:** Scale · Upper mid-market

**Headline:** The CIO is also the person resetting access.

**Dek:** Eight hundred people. No AI staff. A systems landscape held together by a small team who already have jobs. The install has to leave a footprint they can carry, or it should not start.

### A smaller footprint

One process. One binding, read-only for longer than a larger company would tolerate. An eval set that fits on a page, not in a platform. A keeper who already works on the floor and is given hours, not a new department. If the economics of the engagement require a standing committee, we are the wrong firm for this size, and we will say that.

### What we still refuse to skip

The access design, even if it is short. The loop card, even if the release name is the owner. Shadow week, even if it is three days. Weather, even if the "visit" is a written decision and a call. Small is not a reason to return to clock A.

### The keeper they can hire

The handback names a person who exists. "We will hire an AI lead in the second half" is not a name. If the company cannot name the keeper, we can still do the cartography and the recommendation, and we stop before a queue depends on us forever. A mid-market firm should not rent its judgment from a vendor by accident.

### Close

If this is you, say how many people touch the process. The number is the scoping tool. It is more useful than a strategy adjective.
